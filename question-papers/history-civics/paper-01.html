Part I — Question 1

(i) Adopted 26 November 1949; came into force 26 January 1950. (ii) Lok Sabha (House of the People) and Rajya Sabha (Council of States). (iii) Must be a citizen of India and at least 25 years of age. (iv) Right to Equality (Article 17 specifically abolishes untouchability). (v) The President of India. (vi) The Supreme Court of India. (vii) 35 years. (viii) Habeas Corpus. (ix) The President of India. (x) The Gram Panchayat.

✓

Question 2 — The President of India

(a) The President is elected by an Electoral College consisting of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States (and Union Territories with a legislature), through proportional representation by means of a single transferable vote, using a secret ballot.

(b) Any four: appoints the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers; appoints the Chief Justice and other judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts; can grant pardons, reprieves and remissions of punishment (Article 72); summons, prorogues and can dissolve Parliament; is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces; gives assent to bills passed by Parliament.

(c) Article 61; impeachment requires a special majority — two-thirds of the total membership — in each House of Parliament, on a charge of "violation of the Constitution."

✓

Question 3 — Non-Cooperation & Civil Disobedience

(a) The Rowlatt Act (1919) and the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (April 1919), together with the Khilafat issue, angered Indians and led Gandhi to launch the Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920.

(b) Because of the violent Chauri Chaura incident (5 February 1922) in the United Provinces, where a mob set fire to a police station and killed several policemen. Committed to non-violence, Gandhi withdrew the movement once it turned violent.

(c) Gandhi and his followers marched about 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi to make salt from seawater, defying the government's salt monopoly. This launched the Civil Disobedience Movement, drew international attention to India's freedom struggle, and demonstrated a simple, replicable form of mass non-violent resistance that spread across the country.

✓

Question 4 — World War II & the United Nations

(a) Germany's invasion of Poland, on 1 September 1939.

(b) The League lacked its own armed force to enforce decisions; major powers like the USA never joined; it required unanimity, making decisive action difficult; and it failed to stop earlier aggression — Japan's invasion of Manchuria (1931) and Italy's invasion of Abyssinia (1935) — which emboldened Hitler.

(c) The United Nations was formed on 24 October 1945, following the San Francisco Conference, with its headquarters in New York. One of its main objectives is to maintain international peace and security (also accept: promoting friendly relations among nations, or international cooperation on economic, social and humanitarian problems).

✓

Question 5 — Hitler & Nazism

(a) 1933.

(b) Any two: the harsh terms of the Treaty of Versailles (heavy reparations, loss of territory, the "war guilt" clause) created deep resentment; the Great Depression from 1929 caused severe unemployment, which the Nazis exploited by promising recovery and national pride; the political instability of the Weimar Republic, with weak coalition governments, made extremist parties more attractive to voters.

(c) Any two: suppression of political opposition and civil liberties (banning rival parties, controlling the press); persecution of Jews and other minorities, culminating in the Holocaust; a totalitarian state using propaganda and the Gestapo (secret police) to control the population; aggressive rearmament and expansionist foreign policy in violation of the Treaty of Versailles.
